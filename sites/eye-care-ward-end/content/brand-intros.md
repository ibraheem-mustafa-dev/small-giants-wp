## Ray-Ban
Born in 1937 as protection for American pilots, and never really out of fashion since. The Wayfarer and the Aviator are two of the most recognised frames ever made, and both come in sizes and colours to suit most faces.

## Gucci
A Florence fashion house from 1921, known for its green and red stripe, its horsebit and its bold, confident frames. This is the dressy end of the shop, and the one people choose when they want sunglasses to be noticed.

## Oakley
Founded in 1975 and still defined by sport, from cycling to running to golf. Wrap-around shapes, tough lightweight frames and lenses designed for glare make it the practical choice when performance matters as much as looks.

## Prada
Milan, 1913, and a reputation for restraint with a twist. Prada frames tend to be clean, a little unexpected, and quietly unmistakable. Good if you like designer without the shouting.

## Versace
The Medusa head and the Greek key are the signature here, and the frames follow suit: gold detail, strong shapes and plenty of glamour. Made for people who treat sunglasses as part of the outfit.

## Dolce & Gabbana
Two designers, Domenico Dolce and Stefano Gabbana, and a Sicilian sense of drama: lace, leopard, rich colour and a lot of attitude. The frames are decorative and confident, and they photograph well.

## Balenciaga
Cristóbal Balenciaga's house, founded in Spain in 1917, now known for oversized, sculptural and deliberately unusual shapes. If you want a frame that people ask about, this is where to look.

## Michael Kors
Easy American style with a polished finish, usually in neutral tones with a gold-toned logo detail. The frames are comfortable for everyday wear, and they work as well on a school run as they do on a weekend away.

## Polaroid
A name built on polarised lenses, which cut the glare off water, wet roads and car bonnets. The frames are light and straightforward, so this is the one to reach for if driving or holidays are the main reason you're buying.

## Police
Italian sunglasses with an edge: strong lines, metal detailing and a sporty, urban look. A confident choice for people who want a sharper style than the usual classics.

## Carrera
Started in Austria in 1956 with skiers and sportspeople in mind, and its retro shapes still carry that heritage. Think aviators, squares and bold browlines with a vintage feel.

## Ferrari Scuderia
Styling taken from the Ferrari Formula 1 team: the prancing horse, racing red and sleek, sporty lines. These are for people who like their sunglasses to look like they've just come off the grid.

## Emporio Armani
The younger, more contemporary side of Armani, with modern shapes and a relaxed feel. A sensible place to start if you want a recognised name in everyday frames, whether you're buying for the first time or replacing a pair.

## Coach
New York, 1941, started as a maker of leather goods, and the frames keep that polished, classic feel. Rounded shapes, tidy detailing and a signature hardware accent make these easy to wear every day.

## Ralph Lauren
American heritage in eyewear: tortoiseshell, polo-club colours and a classic, slightly preppy look that doesn't date. Frames that suit people who like their style to stay the same year after year.

## Diesel
An Italian denim label from 1978, and the frames have the same irreverent attitude. Chunky acetate, bold colour and the odd surprise in the detail. For people who don't want to look like everyone else.

## Adidas Originals
The three stripes in a relaxed, retro setting. These frames are light, simple and made for everyday wear, so they suit anyone who likes a sporty look without taking it too seriously.

## Barbour
A British name since 1894, known for waxed jackets and country weekends. The eyewear keeps the same understated, practical style, with muted colours and sensible shapes made for the outdoors.

## Calvin Klein
Minimal, clean and modern, as you'd expect from a label that made plain design its signature. The frames are simple, light and easy to match with almost anything.

## Chloé
A Paris house founded in 1952 and known for soft, feminine, slightly bohemian style. Look for gentle curves, delicate detail and colours that flatter, rather than shout.

## David Beckham
Eyewear carrying David Beckham's name, designed with a British, tailored look: clean lines, considered proportions and a smart, grown-up feel. A good fit if you like understated and well-cut.

## DKNY
City style from New York, designed for people who move quickly. Frames are sleek, practical and easy to wear all day, with the logo kept small.

## Dsquared2
The Canadian twins Dean and Dan Caten built this Milan label on playful, high-energy fashion. The frames bring the same edge, with bold shapes and strong detail for people who want some fun.

## Farah
British menswear with a smart-casual feel, and glasses to match. Neat, wearable frames in sensible colours, for people who want to look put together without making a fuss.

## Fila
An Italian sportswear name from 1911, with a retro revival behind it. The frames are lightweight, straightforward and sporty, with a nod to the brand's vintage look.

## Giorgio Armani
The original and most refined Armani line, defined by quiet elegance. The frames are slim, balanced and beautifully finished, which makes them a favourite for people who prefer subtle over showy.

## Hugo Boss
German tailoring brought to glasses: crisp lines, neat proportions and a professional, polished look. Frames that sit comfortably in an office as well as out of one.

## Lipsy
A UK fashion label for younger, trend-led customers, and the frames reflect it. Fun colours, eye-catching shapes and a modern feel make them a good way to add some personality.

## Marc Jacobs
New York designer style with a sense of humour: bold colour, playful shapes and a slightly offbeat finish. A good choice if you want something creative but still wearable.

## MaxMara
Elegance from an Italian house better known for its coats. The frames are refined and feminine, with soft colour and flattering shapes for people who like a classic look.

## Montblanc
Best known for writing instruments, from a Hamburg company founded in 1906, and the six-pointed white star is its mark. The glasses are precise, well-balanced and made from quality materials, aimed at people who value craftsmanship.

## Mulberry
British leather goods from Somerset, made since 1971, and the frames carry the same heritage. Warm colours, classic shapes and a quiet sense of luxury.

## Nike
Sports performance in glasses form. Lightweight, flexible frames made to stay put when you move, whether you're running, cycling or just busy all day.

## O'Neill
A surf brand started in California in 1952, and the relaxed, outdoor spirit shows. These are durable, easygoing frames for beach days, travel and being outside.

## Radley
A London brand known for its Scottie dog, and the frames add a little charm to everyday wear. Neat, feminine shapes and gentle colours that are easy to like.

## Superdry
Cheltenham-born in 2003, with Japanese-inspired graphics and a casual street look. The frames are bold and youthful, and a popular first pair for younger buyers.

## Swarovski
Austrian crystal since 1895, and the sparkle is the point. Expect a touch of crystal on the frame, in elegant shapes made to catch the light.

## Tiffany
New York jeweller founded in 1837, with its unmistakable blue. The frames are delicate and refined, made for people who want something special for a gift or an occasion.

## Tommy Hilfiger
American classics in red, white and navy since 1985. The frames are casual, wearable and easy to like, with a smart, preppy look that works on most people.

## Vogue
Fashion-led frames designed to follow the season. A good pick for a bright, current pair, or for a second pair to keep in the bag.
