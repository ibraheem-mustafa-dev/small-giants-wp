# Exploration Mode: Worked Example

## Setup
Bean says: `/brainstorming explore` then "I keep wondering whether the SGS client reports should read like a person wrote them or like a clean tool output"

## How the session flows

**Opening (no solutions, just questions):**
> "That's an interesting tension. Before we go anywhere with it, what made you start thinking about this now? Has something happened that brought it up?"

Bean: "Yeah, I noticed I write client emails very differently from how I write prompts. In emails I say please and thank you and soften everything. In prompts I just bark instructions."

> "That's a really telling signal. What do you think the softening does in an email: is it about the client, or about you?"

Bean: "Probably about me. Makes it feel less like I'm just issuing orders."

> "So the human-or-tool question might be more about how YOU want to come across than about what the report contains. What would change if the report read like clean tool output and the personal note lived somewhere else?"

## Insights after 5 exchanges

A running list of what's surfaced: discoveries, questions, and contradictions, with how they relate:

- **Insight:** Bean is polite and warm in client emails, direct in prompts. The report-tone question is really about Bean's working style, not the report's content. *(richest thread, several others branch off it)*
- **Connection:** ADHD brains respond better to collaborative framing than command framing (dopamine from social interaction), which connects to the politeness observation.
- **Open question:** If a warmer tone improves client trust, does it also improve how often clients act on the report, or just how it feels to receive?
- **Insight:** The softening in emails emerged naturally. Bean never decided to do it. Tone might work best when it comes from a real person, not from a template. *(richest thread)*
- **Contradiction:** Bean's own brand guidelines fix a formal tone for all client communication, a real tension between a prescribed voice and the natural warmth he actually uses. Contradicts the "emergent" insight above.

The "tone from a real person" thread has sparked the most. It connects to the collaborative-framing point and directly contradicts the formal tone fixed in the brand guidelines. The client-action question is still dangling, so it is worth exploring next.

## Periodic summary (after exchange 5)

> "Here's what's emerging: the richest thread is that tone works best when it comes from a person, not a template, because you soften client emails without deciding to. That directly contradicts your brand guidelines, which fix a formal tone. There's also an open question: does a warmer report get acted on more, or just feel nicer? What else should we dig into?"
