# R3 - A surface whose newest report predates the sweep must be reported, not silently served

## Why this exists

Added 2026-10-06 by Bean's decision. This is the defect that makes a **stale measurement look current** -
directly against the goal of a trustworthy measuring tool.

## Diagnosis

When `scripts/computed-route/solve.mjs` fails, it leaves a directory holding draft caches and a `round-1`
folder but **no `solve-report.json`**.

`scripts/computed-route/lib/sweep.mjs::latestReport` globs `*/solve-report.json`. The incomplete directory
therefore has nothing to match, is skipped, and **the previous run is used instead** - with nothing going
red in either sweep or triage.

**Observed live:** `header` failed with a walker `TimeoutError` during Session C2's sweep, and its 01:37
measurement would have been reported as current had the runner not happened to log the failure. The sweep
cannot rely on someone reading the log.

## The fix

Either is acceptable; pick one and say which:

1. **`sweep.mjs` reports any surface whose newest report predates the sweep's own start** - preferred, since
   it needs no cooperation from the failing writer, and catches a stale report whatever caused it.
2. `solve.mjs` writes a failure marker that `latestReport` refuses.

Option 1 is self-contained in your owned files. **If you choose option 2 you would need `solve.mjs`, which
is not in your set - choose option 1 or report the blocker.**

## Owned files

`scripts/computed-route/lib/sweep.mjs`, tests in `scripts/computed-route/tests/sweep.test.mjs` and
`scripts/computed-route/tests/register-sweep.test.mjs`.

## Negative control

- **Red on revert:** a case with a surface directory holding no `solve-report.json` and an older sibling
  report, asserting the sweep **reports the surface as stale** rather than returning the older report as
  current. Reverting must turn it red.
- **Not over-suppressing:** a surface whose report is **current** must be returned normally, and a sweep in
  which every surface is fresh must report **no** staleness. If the check is too eager, every sweep reports
  false staleness and the signal gets ignored - which returns you to today's behaviour by a different route.
